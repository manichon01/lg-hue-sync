How to use calibration

1. Test colours (easiest)

On the Tuning tab (and under Calibration) pick Red, Green, Blue, White, Warm or Daylight. The lights show that colour as if the TV screen were completely that colour, using the same processing as normal sync. It works from the TV app itself; no HDMI source is needed.

Leave a colour selected and change Saturation, Brightness, Gamma, Highlights or Direct RGB: the lights change straight away. Press Return to sync when you are done.

Fixing blues that look teal or green: select Blue, then try turning Direct RGB off (uses the Hue colour conversion) and lowering Saturation toward 1.0×. Very high saturation darkens pure blue.

2. Capture patterns page (tests the full capture path)

The link Open TV capture patterns opens a full-screen colour page. The lights only react if that page is shown on an HDMI input of the TV, because only HDMI video is captured. Opening it in the TV’s own browser does nothing.

To use it, open http://<tv-address>:8088/capture-patterns in Chrome on a PC or phone and cast that tab to the Google TV (Cast → Cast tab). The page has its own panel at the bottom with Direct RGB, Saturation and Brightness, and shows the colours being sent to each light.

3. Quadrants and 4D tracer

These are Nanoleaf alignment aids and ignore the Tuning settings.
